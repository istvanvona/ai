From edge to bulk in free fermions in disguise

Explicit density correlators from induced paths

Istvan Vona

10 November 2026

Download PDFLaTeX source Paper + code bundle

Abstract

A free-fermion spectrum does not by itself determine local spin correlation functions. For the open free-fermions-in-disguise chain, however, each of the first three Hamiltonian terms, and by reflection each of the last three, has an explicitly computable infinite-temperature dynamical correlator with every term in the chain. We derive these correlators in three steps: the end-to-end density correlator, boundary sources coupled to a clique of up to three terms, and a target moved into the bulk. The mechanism is trace orthogonality: two induced-path products survive precisely when their vertex sets differ by the target vertex. At the endpoint this gives one scalar path polynomial; in the bulk only two terminal configurations and three residual interval polynomials are needed. Using unnormalized spectral residues removes all fermion-normalization factors, while a uniform subtraction accounts for stationary source components without casework. The resulting finite double sums require polynomial arithmetic work in system size. The derivation does not reconstruct a generic bulk operator or solve the bulk–bulk inverse problem.

1 The question and the chain#

Fendley’s open chain has a free-fermion spectrum even though its local terms are not ordinary Jordan–Wigner fermion bilinears [1, 2, 3]. The inverse problem asks for the converse dictionary: which local spin operators can be expressed simply using the hidden modes? Boundary-operator dynamics was obtained by Vona, Mestyán and Pozsgay [4]. The path-product expansion of Fukai, Pozsgay and Vona [5] now makes the operator content of the modes explicit. Here we use that expansion to evaluate density correlators without solving the full inverse problem for the bulk density.

Let

ℋ=∑j=1Mhj,hj=bj⁢gj,Bj=bj2>0,gj2=1,gi⁢gj=(−1)𝟏0<|i−j|≤2⁢gj⁢gi.\mathcal{H}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}h_{j},\qquad h_{j}=b_{j}g_{j},\qquad B_{j}=b_{j}^{2}% >0,\qquad g_{j}^{2}=1,\quad g_{i}g_{j}=(-1)^{\mathbf{1}_{0<|i-j|\leq 2}}g_{j}g% _{i}. (1)

The couplings bjb_{j} may have either sign. The frustration graph has one vertex for each hjh_{j} and an edge for every anticommuting pair: it is the square of an open path, not the ordinary path. We fix the spin realization

gj=Zj−2⁢Zj−1⁢Xj,Z0=Z−1=1,⟨A⟩∞=2−M⁢Tr⁡A,A⁢(t)=ei⁢ℋ⁢t⁢A⁢e−i⁢ℋ⁢t.g_{j}=Z_{j-2}Z_{j-1}X_{j},\qquad Z_{0}=Z_{-1}=1,\qquad\langle A\rangle_{\infty% }=2^{-M}\operatorname{Tr}A,\qquad A(t)=e^{\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}t}Ae^{-\mathrm{% i}\mathcal{H}t}. (2)

The ordered words gA=∏j∈A↑gjg_{A}=\prod_{j\in A}^{\uparrow}g_{j} are trace-orthonormal: ⟨gA†⁢gC⟩∞=δA,C\langle g_{A}^{\dagger}g_{C}\rangle_{\infty}=\delta_{A,C}. Thus a nonempty word has zero trace. This is the trace convention used throughout; no projection onto a particular symmetry sector is made. Our observables are the coupled terms hjh_{j}. Divide ⟨hi⁢(t)⁢hj⟩∞\langle h_{i}(t)h_{j}\rangle_{\infty} by bi⁢bjb_{i}b_{j} to obtain the correlator of gi,gjg_{i},g_{j}.

For an interval, define its weighted independence polynomial by

P[a,b]⁢(x)=∑I⊆[a,b]|i−j|>2⁢(i≠j)(−x)|I|⁢∏i∈IBi,P[a,b]=1(a>b).P_{[a,b]}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[a,b]\\ |i-j|>2\ (i\neq j)\end{subarray}}(-x)^{|I|}\prod_{i\in I}B_{i},\qquad P_{[a,b]% }=1\quad(a>b). (3)

Selecting or omitting the last vertex gives

P[a,m]⁢(x)=P[a,m−1]⁢(x)−x⁢Bm⁢P[a,m−3]⁢(x).P_{[a,m]}(x)=P_{[a,m-1]}(x)-xB_{m}P_{[a,m-3]}(x). (4)

Write P⁢(x)=P[1,M]⁢(x)P(x)=P_{[1,M]}(x) and S=⌈M/3⌉S=\lceil M/3\rceil. We first assume simple roots xa=ua2>0x_{a}=u_{a}^{2}>0, and use signed labels

ℐ={−S,…,−1,1,…,S},u−a=−ua,εs=us−1,Ps′=P′⁢(us2).\mathcal{I}=\{-S,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,S\},\qquad u_{-a}=-u_{a},\quad\varepsilon_% {s}=u_{s}^{-1},\quad P^{\prime}_{s}=P^{\prime}(u_{s}^{2}). (5)

These are the single-particle energies: the spectrum is built from signed sums of the positive εa\varepsilon_{a} [1, 2]. Singular parameter choices are obtained by continuity of the final finite-dimensional correlator, not by substituting into an individual singular residue.

2 A boundary source turns dynamics into residues#

Start with χ1=Z1\chi_{1}=Z_{1}. It anticommutes only with h1h_{1}, so

ℒ⁢(A):=12⁢[ℋ,A],ℒ⁢(χ1)=h1⁢χ1,h1=ℒ⁢(χ1)⁢χ1.\mathcal{L}(A):=\tfrac{1}{2}[\mathcal{H},A],\qquad\mathcal{L}(\chi_{1})=h_{1}% \chi_{1},\qquad h_{1}=\mathcal{L}(\chi_{1})\chi_{1}. (6)

Thus it suffices to evolve the source and its commutator. Add a source vertex α1\alpha_{1} adjacent only to vertex 11. A rooted induced path pp has physical vertices (i1,…,in)(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}), and path word

Πp=χ1⁢hi1⁢⋯⁢hin,Π∅=χ1.\Pi_{p}=\chi_{1}h_{i_{1}}\cdots h_{i_{n}},\qquad\Pi_{\varnothing}=\chi_{1}. (7)

Remove the path and every vertex adjacent to it, including those adjacent to the source. The remaining physical graph is the residual graph p¯\overline{p}. For the empty path it is [2,M][2,M]; for a nonempty path ending at rr it is [r+3,M][r+3,M]. The latter fact follows directly from the gap rule in Fig. 1.

The path-product numerator and its resolvent are

Ξ1⁢(u)=∑p(−u)|p|⁢Pp¯⁢(u2)⁢Πp,Φ1⁢(u)=Ξ1⁢(u)P⁢(u2)=(1−u⁢ℒ)−1⁢χ1.\Xi_{1}(u)=\sum_{p}(-u)^{|p|}P_{\overline{p}}(u^{2})\Pi_{p},\qquad\Phi_{1}(u)=% \frac{\Xi_{1}(u)}{P(u^{2})}=(1-u\mathcal{L})^{-1}\chi_{1}. (8)

Here |p||p| counts physical vertices. The equality is the path-product identity of Ref. [5], specialized to this chain. Its useful local form is

u⁢ℒ⁢Ξ1⁢(u)=Ξ1⁢(u)−P⁢(u2)⁢χ1.u\mathcal{L}\Xi_{1}(u)=\Xi_{1}(u)-P(u^{2})\chi_{1}. (9)

It can be understood without diagonalizing a spin Hamiltonian. In a commutator, an internal path vertex has two path neighbors and hence contributes nothing. The endpoint can be removed or extended; the residual-polynomial recurrence accounts for these two operations. Terms that insert a chord cancel between the corresponding alternative parent paths. The surviving empty-path term is exactly −P⁢(u2)⁢χ1-P(u^{2})\chi_{1}. Ref. [5] proves this cancellation and the associated fermionic algebra; the correlator derivations below require only (9) and the explicit path words.

A normalization-free choice.#

Instead of introducing normalized fermions and their source coefficients separately, take the spectral components of χ1\chi_{1} directly:

Fs(1)=−Ξ1⁢(us)2⁢us2⁢Ps′,ℒ⁢Fs(1)=εs⁢Fs(1).F_{s}^{(1)}=-\frac{\Xi_{1}(u_{s})}{2u_{s}^{2}P^{\prime}_{s}},\qquad\mathcal{L}% F_{s}^{(1)}=\varepsilon_{s}F_{s}^{(1)}. (10)

The sign and factor follow from the residue at u=usu=u_{s}: Res⁡Φ1=Ξ1⁢(us)/(2⁢us⁢Ps′)\operatorname{Res}\Phi_{1}=\Xi_{1}(u_{s})/(2u_{s}P^{\prime}_{s}), whereas Res(1−u/us)−1Fs(1)=−usFs(1)\operatorname{Res}(1-u/u_{s})^{-1}F_{s}^{(1)}=-u_{s}F_{s}^{(1)}. Partial fractions therefore give

χ1⁢(t)=Z1st+∑s∈ℐe2⁢i⁢t⁢εs⁢Fs(1),[ℋ,Z1st]=0,Z1st=χ1−∑sFs(1).\chi_{1}(t)=Z_{1}^{\rm st}+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{I}}e^{2\mathrm{i}t\varepsilon_{s% }}F_{s}^{(1)},\qquad[\mathcal{H},Z_{1}^{\rm st}]=0,\qquad Z_{1}^{\rm st}=\chi_% {1}-\sum_{s}F_{s}^{(1)}. (11)

The superscript distinguishes the stationary operator Z1stZ_{1}^{\rm st} from the Pauli matrix Z1Z_{1}. No explicit construction of this stationary part will be needed.

For comparison with normalized modes, set Q1⁢(x)=P[2,M]⁢(x)Q_{1}(x)=P_{[2,M]}(x), Ns(1)=2⁢−us2⁢Q1⁢(us2)⁢Ps′N_{s}^{(1)}=2\sqrt{-u_{s}^{2}Q_{1}(u_{s}^{2})P^{\prime}_{s}} and Ψs(1)=Ξ1⁢(us)/Ns(1)\Psi_{s}^{(1)}=\Xi_{1}(u_{s})/N_{s}^{(1)}. Then Fs(1)=ds(1)⁢Ψs(1)F_{s}^{(1)}=d_{s}^{(1)}\Psi_{s}^{(1)} with ds(1)=2⁢Q1⁢(us2)/Ns(1)d_{s}^{(1)}=2Q_{1}(u_{s}^{2})/N_{s}^{(1)} and {Ψs(1),Ψr(1)}=δs+r,0\{\Psi_{s}^{(1)},\Psi_{r}^{(1)}\}=\delta_{s+r,0} [5]. Equation (10) is simply this product with its normalization factors cancelled.

3 First calculation: the end-to-end correlator#

3.1 One path polynomial#

Let Rm⁢(y)R_{m}(y) sum all induced paths from 11 to mm, weighting every physical vertex except the final one by −y⁢Bi-yB_{i}:

Rm⁢(y)=∑p:1↝m∏i∈V⁢(p)∖{m}(−y⁢Bi).R_{m}(y)=\sum_{p:1\leadsto m}\prod_{i\in V(p)\setminus\{m\}}(-yB_{i}). (12)

There are only two terminal possibilities: a final jump m−2→mm-2\to m, or a final pair of jumps m−3→m−1→mm-3\to m-1\to m. Consequently

R1=1,R2=R3=−y⁢B1,Rm\displaystyle R_{1}=1,\qquad R_{2}=R_{3}=-yB_{1},\qquad R_{m} =−y⁢Bm−2⁢Rm−2+y2⁢Bm−3⁢Bm−1⁢Rm−3(m≥4).\displaystyle=-yB_{m-2}R_{m-2}+y^{2}B_{m-3}B_{m-1}R_{m-3}\quad(m\geq 4). (13)

For example R4=y2⁢B1⁢(B2+B3)R_{4}=y^{2}B_{1}(B_{2}+B_{3}) and R5=y2⁢B1⁢B3−y3⁢B1⁢B2⁢B4R_{5}=y^{2}B_{1}B_{3}-y^{3}B_{1}B_{2}B_{4}. The terminal coupling BmB_{m} is absent: the observable insertion will supply it.

3.2 Trace orthogonality pairs a path with its extension#

Consider the unnormalized overlap

𝒦1⁢M⁢(u,v):=⟨Ξ1⁢(u)⁢Ξ1⁢(v)⁢hM⟩∞.\mathcal{K}_{1M}(u,v):=\langle\Xi_{1}(u)\Xi_{1}(v)h_{M}\rangle_{\infty}. (14)

In a product of two path words the two source factors disappear. The trace can then be nonzero only if

V⁢(p)△V⁢(q)={M},V(p)\mathbin{\triangle}V(q)=\{M\}, (15)

where △\triangle is symmetric difference. Hence the paths are a common prefix ρ\rho and its one-vertex extension ρ⁢M\rho M. Both residual graphs are empty: the prefix already ends at M−1M-1 or M−2M-2. This is why no right-tail polynomial remains.

To see the sign explicitly, let n=|ρ|n=|\rho| and Bρ=∏i∈ρBiB_{\rho}=\prod_{i\in\rho}B_{i}. The two traces are

⟨Πρ⁢Πρ⁢M⁢hM⟩∞=(−1)n⁢BM⁢Bρ,⟨Πρ⁢M⁢Πρ⁢hM⟩∞=(−1)n−1⁢BM⁢Bρ.\langle\Pi_{\rho}\Pi_{\rho M}h_{M}\rangle_{\infty}=(-1)^{n}B_{M}B_{\rho},% \qquad\langle\Pi_{\rho M}\Pi_{\rho}h_{M}\rangle_{\infty}=(-1)^{n-1}B_{M}B_{% \rho}. (16)

Multiplying by (−u)n⁢(−v)n+1(-u)^{n}(-v)^{n+1} and (−u)n+1⁢(−v)n(-u)^{n+1}(-v)^{n}, respectively, gives BM⁢(u−v)⁢(−u⁢v)n⁢BρB_{M}(u-v)(-uv)^{n}B_{\rho}. Summing the prefixes proves

𝒦1⁢M(u,v)=BM(u−v)RM(uv).\boxed{\mathcal{K}_{1M}(u,v)=B_{M}(u-v)R_{M}(uv).} (17)

This identity holds for arbitrary u,vu,v, not just at spectral roots.

3.3 A uniform treatment of stationary components#

From (6), h1⁢(t)=(∑sεs⁢e2⁢i⁢t⁢εs⁢Fs(1))⁢χ1⁢(t)h_{1}(t)=(\sum_{s}\varepsilon_{s}e^{2\mathrm{i}t\varepsilon_{s}}F_{s}^{(1)})% \chi_{1}(t). The remaining stationary matrix element can be eliminated at time zero. For M>1M>1,

⟨Fs(1)⁢χ1⁢hM⟩∞=0,⟨Fs(1)⁢Z1st⁢hM⟩∞=−∑r⟨Fs(1)⁢Fr(1)⁢hM⟩∞.\langle F_{s}^{(1)}\chi_{1}h_{M}\rangle_{\infty}=0,\qquad\langle F_{s}^{(1)}Z_% {1}^{\rm st}h_{M}\rangle_{\infty}=-\sum_{r}\langle F_{s}^{(1)}F_{r}^{(1)}h_{M}% \rangle_{\infty}. (18)

Indeed, a single source path cannot have physical vertex set {M}\{M\}. The first equality is a selection rule, not an assumption that the stationary part vanishes. Define the common spectral weight

𝒲s⁢r⁢(t)=e2⁢i⁢t⁢(εs+εr)−e2⁢i⁢t⁢εs4⁢us3⁢ur2⁢Ps′⁢Pr′.\mathcal{W}_{sr}(t)=\frac{e^{2\mathrm{i}t(\varepsilon_{s}+\varepsilon_{r})}-e^% {2\mathrm{i}t\varepsilon_{s}}}{4u_{s}^{3}u_{r}^{2}P^{\prime}_{s}P^{\prime}_{r}}. (19)

Equations (10), (17) and (18) yield the first answer:

⟨h1(t)hM⟩∞=BM∑s,r∈ℐ𝒲s⁢r(t)(us−ur)RM(usur),M>1.\boxed{\langle h_{1}(t)h_{M}\rangle_{\infty}=B_{M}\sum_{s,r\in\mathcal{I}}% \mathcal{W}_{sr}(t)(u_{s}-u_{r})R_{M}(u_{s}u_{r}),\qquad M>1.} (20)

When Z1st=0Z_{1}^{\rm st}=0, the subtraction in (19) sums to zero; when it is present, the same subtraction includes its effect exactly. No congruence-class switch is needed. Moreover 𝒲s⁢r⁢(0)=0\mathcal{W}_{sr}(0)=0, so the off-diagonal equal-time answer vanishes term by term. Pairing (s,r)(s,r) with (−s,−r)(-s,-r) replaces the exponentials by cosines, showing that the result is real and even in tt.

For M=2,3M=2,3, the single positive root gives the elementary check

⟨h1⁢(t)⁢hM⟩∞=B1⁢BM∑i=1MBi⁢[1−cos⁡(2⁢t⁢∑i=1MBi)].\langle h_{1}(t)h_{M}\rangle_{\infty}=\frac{B_{1}B_{M}}{\sum_{i=1}^{M}B_{i}}% \left[1-\cos\!\left(2t\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{M}B_{i}}\right)\right]. (21)

For M=1M=1, separately, ⟨h1⁢(t)⁢h1⟩∞=B1\langle h_{1}(t)h_{1}\rangle_{\infty}=B_{1}.

4 The next step: the first three boundary terms#

A source for h2h_{2} alone is not needed. Instead use the nested boundary sums

Kk={1,…,k},Sk=h1+⋯+hk,χk=Z1⁢⋯⁢Zk,ℒ⁢(χk)=Sk⁢χk,1≤k≤min⁡(3,M).K_{k}=\{1,\ldots,k\},\quad S_{k}=h_{1}+\cdots+h_{k},\quad\chi_{k}=Z_{1}\cdots Z% _{k},\quad\mathcal{L}(\chi_{k})=S_{k}\chi_{k},\qquad 1\leq k\leq\min(3,M). (22)

Each KkK_{k} is a simplicial clique: its vertices are mutually adjacent, and for every vertex in KkK_{k} its neighbors outside KkK_{k} form a clique. Thus attaching αk\alpha_{k} to KkK_{k} preserves the graph condition needed for the path-product construction [2, 3, 5]. A path may start at any vertex of KkK_{k}, but it cannot visit another one: that would create a chord back to the source. After leaving the clique, the same monotone gap rule applies.

Replace χ1\chi_{1} and its paths by χk\chi_{k} and paths rooted at αk\alpha_{k} in (8). Denote the result Ξk⁢(u)\Xi_{k}(u). The empty-path residual becomes Qk⁢(x)=P[k+1,M]⁢(x)Q_{k}(x)=P_{[k+1,M]}(x); a nonempty path ending at rr still has residual [r+3,M][r+3,M]. Nothing changes in the spectrum or residue denominator:

Fs(k)=−Ξk⁢(us)2⁢us2⁢Ps′,χk⁢(t)=Zkst+∑se2⁢i⁢t⁢εs⁢Fs(k),Sk⁢(t)=(∑sεs⁢e2⁢i⁢t⁢εs⁢Fs(k))⁢χk⁢(t).F_{s}^{(k)}=-\frac{\Xi_{k}(u_{s})}{2u_{s}^{2}P^{\prime}_{s}},\quad\chi_{k}(t)=% Z_{k}^{\rm st}+\sum_{s}e^{2\mathrm{i}t\varepsilon_{s}}F_{s}^{(k)},\quad S_{k}(% t)=\left(\sum_{s}\varepsilon_{s}e^{2\mathrm{i}t\varepsilon_{s}}F_{s}^{(k)}% \right)\chi_{k}(t). (23)

Different kk generally give different mode operators, even though their energies agree. We compute each clique correlator in its own source basis and only then take differences.

Let Rm(k)R_{m}^{(k)} be the source-to-mm path sum, again excluding the source and the final vertex from the weight. Set Rm(k)=1R_{m}^{(k)}=1 for 1≤m≤k1\leq m\leq k and Rm(k)=0R_{m}^{(k)}=0 for m≤0m\leq 0. For m>km>k, resolve the two final gaps:

ρ2,m(k)⁢(y)\displaystyle\rho_{2,m}^{(k)}(y) =−y⁢Bm−2⁢Rm−2(k)⁢(y),\displaystyle=-yB_{m-2}R_{m-2}^{(k)}(y), (24)
ρ1,m(k)⁢(y)\displaystyle\rho_{1,m}^{(k)}(y) ={−y⁢Bk,m=k+1,y2⁢Bm−3⁢Bm−1⁢Rm−3(k)⁢(y),m≥k+2,\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-yB_{k},&m=k+1,\\ y^{2}B_{m-3}B_{m-1}R_{m-3}^{(k)}(y),&m\geq k+2,\end{cases}

Here an out-of-range factor BiB_{i} with i≤0i\leq 0 means zero. The special first line of the piecewise definition is the direct path αk→k→k+1\alpha_{k}\to k\to k+1. It must not be replaced by a fictitious preceding physical step. Farther into the chain, (24) is exactly the scalar recurrence (13). In particular

R3(2)=−y⁢(B1+B2),R4(3)=−y⁢(B2+B3).R_{3}^{(2)}=-y(B_{1}+B_{2}),\qquad R_{4}^{(3)}=-y(B_{2}+B_{3}). (25)

The endpoint result immediately generalizes by replacing RMR_{M} with RM(k)R_{M}^{(k)} in (20), provided M>kM>k.

Which source has a stationary part?#

It is the source, not just the chain length, that decides this. The resolvent identity implies [5]

(Zkst)2=limx→∞Qk⁢(x)P⁢(x)⁢ 1.(Z_{k}^{\rm st})^{2}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{Q_{k}(x)}{P(x)}\,\mathbf{1}. (26)

Thus Z1st≠0Z_{1}^{\rm st}\neq 0 for M≡0,2(mod3)M\equiv 0,2\pmod{3}; Z2st≠0Z_{2}^{\rm st}\neq 0 only for M≡0(mod3)M\equiv 0\pmod{3}; and Z3st=0Z_{3}^{\rm st}=0. The stationary content therefore depends on which boundary source is chosen. The uniform weight (19) avoids that issue altogether.

5 Moving the target into the bulk#

Let j>kj>k. The same trace selection rule now reads V⁢(p)⁢△⁢V⁢(q)={j}V(p)\triangle V(q)=\{j\}. The target must again be the final vertex of one path. It cannot be removed from the interior: the vertices on either side would have to become adjacent, contradicting inducedness of the longer path. Nor can the first physical vertex be removed, since the next vertex lies outside the source clique. Hence the pair is still (ρ,ρ⁢j)(\rho,\rho j).

What changes is only the residual graph. Define

Lj⁢(x)=P[j+3,M]⁢(x),C2,j⁢(x)=P[j+1,M]⁢(x),C1,j⁢(x)=P[j+2,M]⁢(x).L_{j}(x)=P_{[j+3,M]}(x),\qquad C_{2,j}(x)=P_{[j+1,M]}(x),\qquad C_{1,j}(x)=P_{% [j+2,M]}(x). (27)

The longer path always leaves LjL_{j}. If the shorter path ends at j−2j-2, its residual is C2,jC_{2,j}; if it ends at j−1j-1, its residual is C1,jC_{1,j}. These are the only possibilities, depicted in Fig. 4.

Write

Δa,j⁢(u,v)=u⁢Lj⁢(u2)⁢Ca,j⁢(v2)−v⁢Ca,j⁢(u2)⁢Lj⁢(v2),a=1,2.\Delta_{a,j}(u,v)=uL_{j}(u^{2})C_{a,j}(v^{2})-vC_{a,j}(u^{2})L_{j}(v^{2}),% \qquad a=1,2. (28)

The path-pair overlap is then

𝒦k⁢j(u,v):=⟨Ξk(u)Ξk(v)hj⟩∞=Bj[ρ2,j(k)(uv)Δ2,j(u,v)+ρ1,j(k)(uv)Δ1,j(u,v)],j>k.\boxed{\mathcal{K}_{kj}(u,v):=\langle\Xi_{k}(u)\Xi_{k}(v)h_{j}\rangle_{\infty}% =B_{j}\!\left[\rho_{2,j}^{(k)}(uv)\Delta_{2,j}(u,v)+\rho_{1,j}^{(k)}(uv)\Delta% _{1,j}(u,v)\right],\quad j>k.} (29)

To derive it, repeat the endpoint sign calculation for each prefix ρ\rho. In one ordering the extra factor −u-u belongs to the longer path, whose residual is Lj⁢(u2)L_{j}(u^{2}); the shorter path carries Ca,j⁢(v2)C_{a,j}(v^{2}). The reversed ordering gives the other term with the opposite sign. Summing the common prefix weights gives ρa,j(k)\rho_{a,j}^{(k)}. This proves (29) for arbitrary spectral parameters. It also explains why a single polynomial in u⁢vuv no longer suffices: the two residual tails are evaluated separately at u2u^{2} and v2v^{2}.

Theorem 1 (Boundary-clique–bulk correlator).

For 1≤k≤min⁡(3,M)1\leq k\leq\min(3,M) and j>kj>k, let 𝒲\mathcal{W} be (19), let the path weights be generated by (24), and let the tails be (27). Then

Dk⁢j(t):=⟨Sk(t)hj⟩∞=∑s,r∈ℐ𝒲s⁢r(t)𝒦k⁢j(us,ur).\boxed{D_{kj}(t):=\langle S_{k}(t)h_{j}\rangle_{\infty}=\sum_{s,r\in\mathcal{I% }}\mathcal{W}_{sr}(t)\mathcal{K}_{kj}(u_{s},u_{r}).} (30)
Proof.

Equation (23) reduces the calculation to two source components. Since j∉Kkj\notin K_{k}, no single source path has physical vertex set {j}\{j\}, and therefore ⟨Fs(k)⁢χk⁢hj⟩∞=0\langle F_{s}^{(k)}\chi_{k}h_{j}\rangle_{\infty}=0. Eliminate ZkstZ_{k}^{\rm st} exactly as in (18). Finally,

⟨Fs(k)⁢Fr(k)⁢hj⟩∞=𝒦k⁢j⁢(us,ur)4⁢us2⁢ur2⁢Ps′⁢Pr′.\langle F_{s}^{(k)}F_{r}^{(k)}h_{j}\rangle_{\infty}=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{kj}(u_{% s},u_{r})}{4u_{s}^{2}u_{r}^{2}P^{\prime}_{s}P^{\prime}_{r}}.

Multiplication by εs\varepsilon_{s} and the time phases gives (30). ∎

Two useful checks.#

At j=Mj=M all three tails are 11, so 𝒦k⁢M=BM⁢(u−v)⁢RM(k)⁢(u⁢v)\mathcal{K}_{kM}=B_{M}(u-v)R_{M}^{(k)}(uv), recovering the endpoint calculation. At the other extreme, k=3,j=4k=3,j=4 gives ρ2,4(3)=−y⁢B2\rho_{2,4}^{(3)}=-yB_{2} and ρ1,4(3)=−y⁢B3\rho_{1,4}^{(3)}=-yB_{3}. Thus the formula includes the first site beyond the boundary block; no artificial condition j≥k+3j\geq k+3 is required. The exceptional initial weight in (24) is essential for this extension.

6 Individual generators, reflection, and nearby targets#

With D0⁢j=0D_{0j}=0, taking differences of the nested source sums gives the desired densities:

⟨hm(t)hj⟩∞=Dm⁢j(t)−Dm−1,j(t),m=1,2,3,j≥4.\boxed{\langle h_{m}(t)h_{j}\rangle_{\infty}=D_{mj}(t)-D_{m-1,j}(t),\qquad m=1% ,2,3,\quad j\geq 4.} (31)

Both terms use the same roots of PP, but their path weights are those of their own source cliques. This is the partial inverse problem in its most useful form: the dynamics of the boundary observable is simple, and only the appropriate two-source-component overlap of the bulk observable is needed. We never assert that hjh_{j} itself is quadratic in a fixed set of hidden modes.

For reflected couplings b¯i=bM+1−i\bar{b}_{i}=b_{M+1-i}, write D¯\bar{D} for the same formulas evaluated on the reflected chain. Trace cyclicity gives

⟨hj⁢(t)⁢hM−r+1⟩∞=D¯r,M+1−j⁢(−t)−D¯r−1,M+1−j⁢(−t),r=1,2,3.\langle h_{j}(t)h_{M-r+1}\rangle_{\infty}=\bar{D}_{r,M+1-j}(-t)-\bar{D}_{r-1,M% +1-j}(-t),\qquad r=1,2,3. (32)

Thus the last three terms are equally accessible. In this real representation the correlators are even in time, so the minus sign may also be dropped after evaluation.

Targets inside the source clique.#

For completeness, the phrase “every term” includes j≤kj\leq k. In this case the only surviving pair is the empty path and the one-vertex path (j)(j). Consequently the kernel is instead

𝒦k⁢j⁢(u,v)=Bj⁢[u⁢Lj⁢(u2)⁢Qk⁢(v2)−v⁢Qk⁢(u2)⁢Lj⁢(v2)],j≤k.\mathcal{K}_{kj}(u,v)=B_{j}\left[uL_{j}(u^{2})Q_{k}(v^{2})-vQ_{k}(u^{2})L_{j}(% v^{2})\right],\qquad j\leq k. (33)

Now ⟨Ξk⁢(u)⁢χk⁢hj⟩∞=u⁢Bj⁢Lj⁢(u2)\langle\Xi_{k}(u)\chi_{k}h_{j}\rangle_{\infty}=uB_{j}L_{j}(u^{2}) is nonzero. Keeping this elementary term when eliminating the stationary part gives

Dk⁢j⁢(t)=∑s,r𝒲s⁢r⁢(t)⁢𝒦k⁢j⁢(us,ur)−Bj2⁢∑sLj⁢(us2)us2⁢Ps′⁢e2⁢i⁢t/us,j≤k.D_{kj}(t)=\sum_{s,r}\mathcal{W}_{sr}(t)\mathcal{K}_{kj}(u_{s},u_{r})-\frac{B_{% j}}{2}\sum_{s}\frac{L_{j}(u_{s}^{2})}{u_{s}^{2}P^{\prime}_{s}}e^{2\mathrm{i}t/% u_{s}},\qquad j\leq k. (34)

Equations (30) and (34), followed by the same finite difference, therefore cover every target 1≤j≤M1\leq j\leq M, including autocorrelations. At t=0t=0 they give Dk⁢j⁢(0)=Bj⁢𝟏j≤kD_{kj}(0)=B_{j}\mathbf{1}_{j\leq k}.

Opposite boundary blocks need no new machinery.#

For Tℓ={M−ℓ+1,…,M}T_{\ell}=\{M-\ell+1,\ldots,M\} and M≥6M\geq 6, summing (29) over the target block gives

⟨Ξk⁢(u)⁢Ξk⁢(v)⁢∑j∈Tℓhj⟩∞\displaystyle\langle\Xi_{k}(u)\Xi_{k}(v)\sum_{j\in T_{\ell}}h_{j}\rangle_{\infty} =(u−v)⁢ℛk⁢ℓ⁢(u⁢v),\displaystyle=(u-v)\mathcal{R}_{k\ell}(uv),
ℛk⁢1⁢(y)\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k1}(y) =BM⁢RM(k)⁢(y),\displaystyle=B_{M}R_{M}^{(k)}(y),
ℛk⁢2⁢(y)\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k2}(y) =BM−1⁢RM−1(k)⁢(y)−y⁢BM⁢BM−2⁢RM−2(k)⁢(y),\displaystyle=B_{M-1}R_{M-1}^{(k)}(y)-yB_{M}B_{M-2}R_{M-2}^{(k)}(y),
ℛk⁢3⁢(y)\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k3}(y) =BM−2⁢RM−2(k)⁢(y)−y⁢BM−1⁢BM−3⁢RM−3(k)⁢(y).\displaystyle=B_{M-2}R_{M-2}^{(k)}(y)-yB_{M-1}B_{M-3}R_{M-3}^{(k)}(y). (35)

These are first-hit path sums: the first vertex encountered in the target clique carries BjB_{j}, while each preceding vertex carries −y⁢Bi-yB_{i}. An induced path can first enter T2T_{2} at M−1M-1, or jump from M−2M-2 to MM; it can first enter T3T_{3} at M−2M-2, or jump from M−3M-3 to M−1M-1. These are exactly the displayed terms. In the overlap sum, contributions that continue inside the clique cancel the residual-tail terms. Equivalently, substitution of the degree-zero and degree-one endpoint tails into (29), followed by (24), verifies all three identities directly. The correlator is ∑s,r𝒲s⁢r⁢(t)⁢(us−ur)⁢ℛk⁢ℓ⁢(us⁢ur)\sum_{s,r}\mathcal{W}_{sr}(t)(u_{s}-u_{r})\mathcal{R}_{k\ell}(u_{s}u_{r}); differences in both block sizes isolate any of the nine opposite-edge pairs.

7 Computation, checks, and the extent of the result#

Polynomial work, without enumerating paths.#

The path expansion is a proof device, not the computational algorithm. Build PP by (4) and determine its S=O⁢(M)S=O(M) roots. At each root, generate all right tails by the backward recurrence

P[a,M]⁢(x)=P[a+1,M]⁢(x)−x⁢Ba⁢P[a+3,M]⁢(x).P_{[a,M]}(x)=P_{[a+1,M]}(x)-xB_{a}P_{[a+3,M]}(x). (36)

For each signed pair (s,r)(s,r), run the scalar recurrence (24) at y=us⁢ury=u_{s}u_{r}, evaluate the desired kernel, and sum (30). For a fixed edge–target pair this takes O⁢(M⁢S2)=O⁢(M3)O(MS^{2})=O(M^{3}) arithmetic operations after the spectral data are obtained; storing its O⁢(S2)O(S^{2}) coefficients reduces each additional time evaluation to O⁢(S2)O(S^{2}). All targets can share the same path sweeps. A conventional polynomial-coefficient/root-finding implementation also has polynomial arithmetic cost. These are arithmetic bounds, not uniform bit-complexity or floating-point stability guarantees: large chains, strongly separated couplings, or clustered roots may require higher precision.

Independent verification.#

The path recurrence, resolvent identity, all-target overlap, and opposite-clique sums were checked by enumerating induced paths and multiplying graph-Clifford words in exact integer arithmetic, through M=18M=18 (102, 51, 509, and 117 checks, respectively). The finite-time formulas were separately compared with direct commutator evolution on the ordered-word basis for 2≤M≤122\leq M\leq 12, all allowed k,jk,j, uniform, nonuniform and random signed couplings, and four times. Across 2,748 scalar comparisons the maximum absolute discrepancy was 1.05×10−131.05\times 10^{-13}. Reproducible scripts and parameter choices accompany the source files. These checks support, but do not replace, the analytic derivation. Useful implementation tests are

⟨hi⁢hj⟩∞=Bi⁢δi⁢j,∑j=1M⟨hi⁢(t)⁢hj⟩∞=Bi,\langle h_{i}h_{j}\rangle_{\infty}=B_{i}\delta_{ij},\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{M}% \langle h_{i}(t)h_{j}\rangle_{\infty}=B_{i}, (37)

the second following from conservation of ℋ\mathcal{H}.

Relation to existing methods.#

The spectral polynomial and hidden fermions originate in Refs. [1, 2, 3]; boundary inverse formulas and selected real-time correlators were obtained in Ref. [4]. The essential input here is the explicit path-product resolvent of Ref. [5], whose general proof is not repeated. The additional reduction is at the level of the density overlap: trace orthogonality leaves two terminal path configurations, and hence the scalar formulas (29)–(30).

Polynomial contraction is also consistent with the matrix-product-operator approach to mode expectation values developed for FFD circuits in Ref. [6]. In the Hamiltonian setting, the finite-range independent-set transfer recurrence has a finite-memory tensor-network representation, so a fixed product of mode operators with a local insertion can likewise be contracted with polynomial work. This gives an alternative complexity argument; the path calculation provides the explicit scalar kernel rather than a claim of first establishing polynomial simulability for boundary observables. Recent work on bulk generalized-Gibbs-ensemble expectations and quench dynamics [7] addresses a different question from the finite-time, infinite-temperature cross-correlators considered here. The distinction between the physical-mode algebra and degeneracy degrees of freedom is developed in Ref. [8].

What is not solved.#

The three-site reach comes from the nested boundary cliques, not from a claim that all densities have a quadratic expression in one common fermion basis. The block {1,2,3,4}\{1,2,3,4\} is not a clique, so the same argument does not produce S4S_{4}. Attaching a source only to a bulk site generally creates a claw, as Fig. 3(b) shows. Thus, when both densities lie outside the accessible edge blocks, a correlator such as ⟨h4⁢(t)⁢hM−3⟩∞\langle h_{4}(t)h_{M-3}\rangle_{\infty} for M≥10M\geq 10 is not determined by these formulas. This is a limitation of the construction, not an impossibility theorem for bulk–bulk dynamics. In particular, no truncation of a generic bulk operator to quadratic or quartic physical-fermion sectors has been assumed.

The central lesson is that a correlation function can be simpler than the inverse problem for either of its factors in a fixed fermionic dictionary. A boundary source supplies the dynamics; the normalized trace selects a small part of the target operator; and induced-path geometry evaluates that part with scalar recurrences. This is precisely the partial inverse problem solved here.
